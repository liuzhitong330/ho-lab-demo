Where does PP2A inhibition change the bottleneck in checkpoint-resistant glioblastoma?

A paper-grounded evidence map suggests that LB-100 does not simply accelerate T-cell growth; it increases inflammatory signaling, reveals an adaptive PD-L1 brake, and makes that brake actionable with PD-1 blockade.

By Cathy Liu · prepared for the Ho Lab, UCSF

The thesis

In the Ho Lab's orthotopic GL261 studies, PP2A inhibition alone did not extend median survival. Its value appeared when paired with PD-1 blockade: the combination increased cytotoxic T-cell entry and function, produced complete tumor regression in a subset of mice, and generated antigen-specific memory. The interaction below separates that mechanism into the claim, the intervention, and the evidence needed to support it.

02 · Mechanism

PP2A inhibition appears to expose a targetable feedback loop

The study's transwell experiment is the hinge. LB-100 increased T-cell IFN-γ secretion; nearby GL261 cells then increased PD-L1. Blocking IFN-γ abolished that induction, while LB-100 applied to GL261 cells without T cells did not change PD-L1.

Interpretation. This is a sensitization model, not a simple additive model. LB-100 creates more antitumor activity and, simultaneously, a compensatory checkpoint. Anti-PD-1 is most useful precisely because that adaptive checkpoint is present.

03 · Causal checks

Three perturbations test whether the story holds

Choose a perturbation to see which link it tests and what happened.

Dependency confirmed

Depleting CD4+, CD8+, or both populations removed the combination's survival benefit.

The treatment effect therefore required both arms of the adaptive T-cell response; it was not explained by a tumor-cell-only action of LB-100.

04 · What persists

The contrast matters: memory CD8+ T cells from cured mice increased IFN-γ when exposed to GL261, but not to the unrelated B16 melanoma line. That specificity is stronger evidence for learned antitumor immunity than survival alone.

05 · A next experiment

Map when an inflamed tumor becomes an exhausted one

A useful extension would combine longitudinal BLI with matched early and late tumor harvests, then profile both lymphoid and myeloid compartments. The central question is temporal: after LB-100 raises inflammatory output, when do microglia and tumor-associated macrophages begin to reinforce immune suppression?

  1. Measure serial BLI and define response trajectories before endpoint divergence.
  2. Profile CD8 state, Tregs, microglia, and macrophage programs at matched tumor burdens.
  3. Test whether early IFN-γ/PD-L1 changes predict durable response or later compensatory myeloid suppression.

This experiment connects the paper's T-cell mechanism to the Ho Lab position's focus on immune evasion and the tumor-associated macrophage/microglial niche. It also fits my experience with orthotopic luciferase-tagged glioblastoma models, longitudinal IVIS imaging, tissue collection, cell culture, flow cytometry, and computational analysis.

Methods and limits

A structured reading, not a raw-data reanalysis

This demo organizes values and causal tests reported in Maggio et al. (2020). No individual mouse-level dataset was available, so no trajectories, confidence intervals, or unreported effect sizes were inferred from figure pixels. The work is preclinical and does not establish human efficacy. The mechanism diagram distinguishes paper-supported interventions from my interpretation of their relationship.
